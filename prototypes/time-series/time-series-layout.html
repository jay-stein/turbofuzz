Six layout rules

The chart never moves

Switching mode keeps the chart's place, zoom and crosshair. People lose the thread when the picture jumps.

Controls on the chart's edge

At most five in the toolbar. Anything rarer goes in the detail section next to what it explains (trend flexibility sits beside the leaderboard).

Summary before detail

Sentence, strip, chart, evidence. Each step is optional; someone can stop at the strip and still have the answer.

One time axis

Everything under the chart shares its x scale and crosshair, so the eye reads straight down from a spike to its remainder or its effect.

One colour per mode

Blue for explore and decompose, violet for forecast, teal for impact. Red and green only carry meaning: unusual, above, below.

Drag, don't fill in forms

Split line, horizon and event date are handles on the chart; a click on any period opens its rows. The toolbar mirrors them for keyboard users.

Page two: the brief

The workbench is for the person doing the analysis. The brief is for the person who reads the answer. Keeping them apart stops the workbench growing presentation clutter.

Built from the workbench, not edited by hand. One click on Export → Brief takes the current mode and makes a single page:

  • the series sentence as the title, the date range and data source as the subtitle;
  • three headline numbers (for example latest, forecast for next quarter, event impact);
  • one chart: the mode you were in, at the zoom you chose, with direct labels;
  • the top three findings as sentences;
  • a footnote with the method, the backtest check and the cautions from the model card.

Exports as PNG and PDF, or as a link that reopens the workbench in the same state. Same light and dark themes, no controls.

What this layout rules out

  • A settings drawer that hides the choices that change the answer, like the aggregate and the gap rule.
  • A grid of eight charts. One chart plus aligned panels; more charts only as small multiples of one kind.
  • A wizard ("step 1 of 4") before the first chart. The page opens on a sensible default series.
  • Legend boxes on the chart. Lines are labelled at their ends; the legend row is a key, not a control.
  • Mode switches that reset zoom or move the chart.
  • Model jargon in the main view. Behaviour names first ("Smooth and damp"), method names in grey beneath.
  • Any view, preset or wording for a particular kind of data. The page reads only the numbers: their shape (zeros, negatives, gaps, cycles), never what they measure.

Two choices for you. Whether the brief ships with the first release or after it, and whether "Impact" is the right tab name (alternatives: "Events", "What changed").
